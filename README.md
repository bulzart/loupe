# Loupe

The web app of the video pipelines (`/analyze-then-pick`, `/analyze-then-generate` and `/analyze-with-loupe`; the runs of all three can be browsed here). It does three things:

1. **Flag record.** Every prompt and video Higgsfield flagged, refused, withheld or rerouted, with the evidence the pipeline could
   gather (the request, each input with its file, thumbnail and file facts, the same file in earlier runs, the raw answer the
   hook saved). It replaces the old `caught_flags.html` page.
2. **Generation misses.** One page, no second step: you pick a run and a video, the frames play side by side (two to start with: the miss from Video A and the right version from Video B, on the same time; either can also be the original video the generation was made from, or a take of another video or run; choosing the right version later, even after marks were drawn on the first frame, adds its frame once at the same time, and the marks stay on the frame they were drawn on), and you
   draw an arrow, circle, box or note straight on any of them (red on a frame you call Wrong, green on a frame you call Right). You choose what each
   frame is: Wrong or Right, which video it comes from (so an early frame of one video can be the right one), and its own time (slider, 1-frame buttons or a time box); **+ Add a frame** adds as many as you need.
   Nothing is marked or copied for you. Undo / Redo / Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y work. Everything is optional: a right version, a wrong frame, even a video or any frame (a report can be in words only: the idea, what could have been better, what the AI agents missed or could not understand).
   Type what the miss was or what the scenario is about,
   press **Mark this moment** (it saves at once), and Loupe saves one image: each frame
   tagged WRONG, RIGHT or EXAMPLE with its time, where it comes from and your marks, a zoom of each frame you marked with a circle, arrow or box (cut around that frame's own marks; a frame with no such mark has none), and your explanation burned in at the bottom. The bottom band is split in half: the explanation on
   the left and, on the right, the **product sample the video used**, found automatically from the run (its request, product registry and asset
   inventory) as the original file in the client's reference folder. The report page shows what it found and lets you switch it off. A miss does not need a right version:
   the page never guesses the right version: you pick it, or pick "No right version exists", before a moment can be saved, and then the miss stays as a miss example (the right panel says so, only the miss is marked). Attach a right version
   later and the saved moments are rebuilt with it (open Edit on each to line the right video up).
3. **QC samples.** The QC phase of a run (and only that phase) reads those images. A separate read-only Claude session, the *keeper*,
   holds the whole library and answers QC agents' questions about it; it never edits anything.

Standard library Python + SQLite + Pillow + ffmpeg. The UI is plain JavaScript (no innerHTML). It listens on `127.0.0.1:8787` only.

## Start

```
python C:/Users/Glaresquare1/.claude/loupe/loupe.py serve         # http://127.0.0.1:8787
```

`atp.py loupe ensure` (or `atg.py loupe ensure`) starts it detached when a pipeline command needs it. The first time you open the page it asks you to **set
the admin passphrase yourself** (8+ characters). Agents never get that passphrase.

## Who may do what

| Who | How | Scopes |
|---|---|---|
| You | passphrase login (12 h session cookie, CSRF header, Host check, 5 wrong tries lock the login for a minute) | everything |
| The pipeline (`atp.py`, `atg.py`, hooks) | `Authorization: Bearer lk_…` from `data/keys/pipeline.key` | `flags:write`, `flags:read`, `runs:read` |
| The QC phase (`qc-consult` of either script, MCP) | `data/keys/qc.key` | `qc:read`, `flags:read` |

Keys are shown once, stored hashed, revocable, with a last-used time and a use counter; logins, key changes, flag and miss edits, settings
and uploads go to the audit log (Settings page); consults are logged on the Keeper page. Make more with `python loupe.py key-create --name N --scopes a,b [--file NAME]`.

## How agents reach it

- **CLI (primary):** `caught-flag add|set|list|reconcile|open`, `qc-consult`, `qc-brief`, `plan-hints` and `loupe ensure|status|open`, with the same arguments in `atp.py`, `atg.py` and `awl.py` (each writes the runs of its own library). `awl.py` also reads the keeper's state (`GET /api/qc/keeper/state`, qc key) in its `status` and `doctor`, and uploads from a capture (`hf-put --capture`).
- **MCP (optional):** `mcp_server.py` offers `qc_search`, `qc_get_sample` (text + the image), `qc_brief`, `qc_consult`, `flags_search`.
  Register once with `claude mcp add --scope user loupe -- python C:/Users/Glaresquare1/.claude/loupe/mcp_server.py`
  (it reads `data/keys/qc.key`, or `LOUPE_API_KEY` if you pass `-e LOUPE_API_KEY=<key>`).
- **HTTP:** the same JSON API the web page uses (`/api/...`, listed in `loupe_app/server.py`).

**Kinds suggested, confirmed by the admin.** `GET /api/misses/suggest-kinds?text=…` returns the kinds of the vocabulary whose own words occur in a text (a prefix match on the vocabulary's words, nothing weighed); the report form's button "Suggest kinds from the words" ticks them and the admin unticks what does not fit; nothing is saved without the admin.

**The composite says what its tags mean.** When the frames come from two sources, or from another video or run, the bottom band opens with a legend, one clause per tag (frames tagged "as generated" are from video A, "correct version" from video B, a different video, "original video" from the original the generation was made from, not generated; "… · video 04" or "… · run R video 03" says where a take of another video or run was taken from); it reads exactly as before for the two usual tags. A frame gets a zoom only when you marked it with a circle, an arrow or a box, cut around its own marks: a frame with no mark, or with a text label only, has none (its place under the row stays empty, and a row with no zoom has no zoom row), and neither has a frame whose own marks cover more than 60 % of it (it would repeat the panel; the band says so). A source too long for its panel shrinks the tag's letters, then is shortened, so the time always shows. Existing samples keep their images until they are regenerated.

**Edited where it stands; the product sample ticked by the admin (since 2026-10-28).** A report and its samples are changed in place, never started again: a sample opened from the reports list, or with **Edit frames and marks** on the QC samples page (`&moment=` in the workspace's address), comes back with its frames, their times and roles, its marks and its words, ready for **Update moment**; a report with one sample opens it that way; **Save report changes** saves the report's own fields (what the miss or scenario is about, the kinds, the videos, the product sample, the prompts, model, mode, product) and rebuilds every image of the report without marking a new moment; and the QC samples page edits the report's title beside the sample's words. The product sample is ticked by the admin from every image the generation of Video A's take used (`GET /api/generation-assets`, `runs.generation_assets`: for each image input of that attempt's video request, the client's original, the file sent to the model and the file the video's uploads record names, each once, with what the request calls it and a thumbnail; the automatic pick marked and ticked). The ticked files, up to four, are kept with the report (`misses.sample_files`, added by a migration; a file that is not one of that generation's images is refused) and go into its images; untouched, the automatic pick is used. A frame whose video the browser cannot play is shown as stills that Loupe cuts (`/api/frame`), so it can still be moved, marked and saved; a time past the end of a shorter take is brought back to its last frame; a frame asked for near the end of a file whose sound runs longer than its picture steps back to the nearest picture; and a take whose run cannot be read keeps its side's tag instead of stopping the save.

## The keeper

The keeper is the judge (the user, 2026-10-28). `qc.py` starts `claude -p … --model claude-opus-5-5 --effort high --tools Read --strict-mcp-config`
(`keeper_model` on the Keeper page, Opus 5.5 unless the admin sets another) as a session of its own (`--session-id`, then `--resume`), working in
`data/keeper`, told the whole library digest. It can read the sample images and write nothing. It alone judges: what went wrong, why, what a check
compares, which prompt did better, what a template should say; the data collector (below) only describes the pictures. After 30 turns it is replaced by a
fresh session rebuilt from the library (the library is the memory, so nothing is lost). If it cannot run, `qc-consult` falls back to the deterministic
brief (`mode: fallback`). A consult on Opus costs more than it did on Haiku and grows with the library and the session's length; each consult's cost is
kept in the `consults` table of the database. Every judge and collector call is started with `--effort` at high or above (`keeper_effort` on the Keeper
page: high, xhigh or max; a lower value is refused, and one stored around the API runs at high), because the Haiku collector's own default is medium;
the CLI still runs a model that has no effort levels.

**What an answer carries (since 2026-10-20).** Every `relevant[]` item names the sample's `title` (the admin's words), `details`, `correct_note`, `idea`, `better`, `missed`, where it came from (`model`, `run`, `video`, `t_wrong`, `kinds`), the composite's path and the keeper's `why`, `check` and `wrong_pattern`. Every field is whole or cut at a sentence end with an ellipsis (`_clip`), never mid-word; `needs_unmet` lists only what Loupe could not fetch, and its `more` item names the kinds and times the keeper still wanted. A run that is not under Loupe's run folders is named as such in the fetch reason.

**The prompts of the two takes (since 2026-10-20).** Beside Video A and Video B the report form keeps the prompt that produced the wrong take and the prompt of the corrected take (each filled from the run's request file of the attempt the version picked belongs to, or pasted) and one line of the admin's own words on what in the prompt made the difference. They live in the `misses` table only (`wrong_prompt`, `fixed_prompt`, `prompt_note`; the seed carries them): never in the composite, its meta or the keeper's digest, and nothing compares or diffs them in code. The judge reads both whole, never cut (the user, 2026-10-28): when it asks for `sample_origin`, in a template request for the samples it chooses, and, before any QC or planning answer is final, for every sample the answer cites that it has not read in that consult (`prompts_judged`); it judges itself what the corrected prompt did differently. The data collector never sees them. A pipeline agent reads them through `GET /api/qc/samples/S<id>?prompts=1`: `awl.py qc-consult`, `plan-hints` and `prompt-template` write both into the run for every sample the answer rests on (`prompts_files`), and `awl.py sample-prompts` fetches any other one. Every list, brief, consult answer and hint carries only `prompts` (`pair`, `wrong`, `fixed` or empty) and `prompt_note`, so the keeper's memory and the agents' context stay small; a change to the prompts changes the library version, so the keeper is re-synced and re-reads them on request.

**A take made of several generations (since 2026-10-28).** A video joined from clips (`60-outputs/video-NN/c1/`, `c2/` …) was made by one generation per clip, each with its own request. `runs.prompt_segments` reads such a take (its clean video, preview, deliverable or `joined.mp4`) as one segment per clip in clip order: the clip, the attempt it used (`result.json` `jobs[].attempts`, else the highest attempt folder with a file), its request (`video-NN-cK[-aJ]-request.json`, else the request whose `clip` key names it), its prompt and the seconds of the take it made, each clip as long as its own file in the join (`trimmed.mp4`, else `conformed.mp4`, else `raw.mp4`); the `note` gives each span and says when the clips add up to another length than the file. A take of one clip gives that clip's own request, so a take of `c2` is never given `c1`'s prompt. `GET /api/request-prompt` answers a joined take with `segments`, and the product sample offers the images of every clip's request. The report form keeps one prompt field per side (the user, 2026-10-28). Video A's field holds the prompt behind the frames called Wrong, and Video B's the prompt behind the frames called Right. With no such frame it uses the frames taken from that side's video, and with none at all the whole take; a Right frame taken from Video A brings Video A's generation into Video B's field. For a joined take, only the generations those frames come from are written in, one after the other, each under its line, which names the frames it is responsible for as the sample's image tags them: `This is the prompt responsible for Frame 1 (WRONG, 0:03.00) and Frame 3 (WRONG, 0:05.20) of this sample: it generated second 0 to second 6.1 of Video A (generation 1 of 2 · clip c1 · video-02-c1-request.json).` (with no frame of the field from it: `This is the prompt that generated … ; no frame of this sample comes from it.`) A field fills itself while it is empty or still holds what Loupe wrote, and follows the frames as they move or change role or video. Once typed in, it is the admin's own until **Fill from the run** writes it again. The field is saved as shown (`wrong_prompt`, `fixed_prompt`). While Loupe's text stands, the take's generations are saved beside it (`wrong_segments`, `fixed_segments`: `src`, clip, attempt, request, `t_from`, `t_to`, prompt), so a saved field comes back following the frames from the prompts it was saved with. A field typed by hand is saved as typed, with none. For the judge, `misses.prompts_of` gives, sample by sample, the prompt of each generation that sample's frames come from (all of them when none does), each under the same line naming that sample's frames. This reaches the sample route with `?prompts=1`, `sample_origin` and the template and QC rounds; the digest says how many generations a take was made of. `awl.py sample-prompts` writes the same into the run's prompts file.

**A frame ties back to its prompt (since 2026-10-28).** The user: the prompt named Video A, but nothing in the saved image tied a frame to it. The sample's image now numbers every frame and names its video. Each tag has two lines. The top line is `FRAME 1 · WRONG · 0:03.00`, in the role's colour. Under it, on a dark ground, comes `VIDEO A · as generated · generation 1 of 2 · clip c1 (0–6.1 s)`: the video, where it comes from and, for a take made of several generations, the generation that made it. A take of one clip says only `clip c2`. The line shrinks and then shortens the source words, so `VIDEO A` always shows. When a frame carries a generation, a legend line says that the prompt of that generation is the one that names the frame. `misses.regenerate` reads the generations from the ones kept with the prompt fields, else from the run (`runs.prompt_segments`), and keeps them with each frame. The frame entries in `meta.json` carry `n`, `video` and `generation`; the moment's stored frames carry `video` and `gen`. The keeper's digest and the brief name each frame the same way: `Frame 1 WRONG 0:03.00, Video A (as generated), generation 1 of 2 · clip c1 (0–6.1 s)`. The keeper's role and the collector's say what the tags mean. The rows sit 30 px lower under the two-line tags; a moment built before this keeps its old tags until it is rebuilt (**Update moment**, or **Save report changes** for every sample of the report).

**Example prompts (since 2026-10-28).** The user: when a sample has no Wrong or Right frame, only Examples, its filled prompts must be marked Example, not wrong and not corrected, so the judge and the keeper are not misled. A field's prompt stands for the frames it speaks for: Video A's for the frames called Wrong, Video B's for those called Right. With none, each field speaks for the frames called Example taken from its own video (no longer for any frame of its video), and its prompt is then an **Example prompt**. When every frame of the sample is an Example, both prompts are. The form's labels say so: `Prompt of Video A (an example take: its frames are called Example, neither wrong nor right)`. The prompt lines name the frames `EXAMPLE`. `misses.prompt_roles` applies the same rule to a sample's frames: per side, `example`, or else `flawed` (Video A) and `correct` (Video B). Moments carry it as `prompt_roles`, and the sample route with `?prompts=1` adds `examples_only`. Each place then names an Example prompt as an example take:
- the judge's text: `the prompt of the example take of Video A (…: it is neither the wrong take nor the corrected one)`, opened by a line when the frames are all Examples;
- the keeper's digest: `PROMPTS OF BOTH EXAMPLE TAKES SAVED`, or `PROMPTS SAVED: THE EXAMPLE TAKE OF VIDEO A AND THE CORRECTED TAKE`;
- the brief: `Video A's prompt, an example take…`;
- the consult's relevant items, the planning hints and the template's basis (`prompt_roles`);
- the samples page chip and the reports list;
- the keeper's role;
- `awl.py sample-prompts`: headings `## Prompt of the example take of Video A`, and a note for a sample of Examples only.

A pair with a Wrong frame keeps both takes. **What each prompt is, in the database and everywhere (since 2026-10-28):** the report keeps `wrong_prompt_role` (`flawed` or `example`) and `fixed_prompt_role` (`correct` or `example`). `misses.sync_prompt_roles` writes them on every save of the report or of a sample, from its samples: `example` when that side's prompt is an Example's in every sample. A report saved before gets them on its first read, and `GET /api/misses` serves them as `prompt_roles`. Unless it is an Example's, Video A's prompt is named, wherever a person or an agent reads it, as the one that generated the flawed version, and Video B's as the correct take's. That covers the form's labels (`Prompt of Video A (as generated: the prompt that generated the flawed version)`, `Prompt of Video B (the correct take)`), the judge's text (`the prompt of the flawed take (Video A: its prompt generated the flawed version)`), the digest (`PROMPTS OF BOTH TAKES SAVED: VIDEO A'S GENERATED THE FLAWED VERSION, VIDEO B'S IS THE CORRECT TAKE`), the brief, the keeper's role and template request, the collector's note, the samples page, the reports list, and the toolkit's consult and hint rules and prompts file (`## Prompt of the flawed take (Video A: …)`, `## Prompt of the correct take (Video B)`). An Example's prompt is named everywhere as just an example, neither flawed nor correct.

**The versions of a video (since 2026-10-20).** `GET /api/runs/<run>` serves each video's files with a `role`: `generated` (the video as the service returned it: `raw.mp4` of an attempt folder `60-outputs/video-NN/aK/`, or the oldest file of an attempt folder that has none; the clean video and the preview are made from it), `processed` (conformed, trimmed, with the original audio, copies, previews, the loose files of `60-outputs/video-NN/`), `deliverable` or `client-fix`. A file of an attempt folder carries its `attempt`, and `delivered` is true for the attempt whose finished file is byte for byte the run's clean video (`clean.mp4` or `deliverables/video-NN-clean.mp4`; sizes are compared first, a hash is read only for equal sizes). The two pickers of the report form list only the generated takes (named like `a3 · generated at Higgsfield · delivered (its clean video and preview)`), the client's fixes, a file uploaded or typed, and whatever an opened report already uses; a tick under them lists every other file as well, and a video with no recognisable generated take lists every file as before. Video A starts on the first attempt's generated take. `GET /api/request-prompt` and the product sample read the attempt from the picked file's path: the attempt folder it sits in, else the attempt it is a copy of (so a clean video fills the prompt of the take it was made from, whatever its name says); a preview, or a deliverable, is the delivered take's when it is the run's own recorded clean or preview of that video (`state.json` `videos[].clean` / `preview`, `result.json`) or a byte-for-byte copy of one; a file whose attempt cannot be told fills nothing and the form says why (nothing is guessed, never "the latest attempt"); a label such as `a2 · raw.mp4` is read by its attempt word. A request file that is not a video call is never the prompt of an attempt: the request of a still asked for beside the video (a start frame, a guide plate) has a `tool` that is not a video call, or no duration and an image model's own parameter (a resolution tier such as 2k, a quality). The usage index labels such a request with its own tag (`g1`) or `image` instead of `a1`, and rebuilds itself once for every run after this change (`USAGE_INDEX_VERSION`).

**Either side can be any video (since 2026-10-27).** Video A and Video B each list, besides the video's own generated takes, the **original video** the generation was made from and the **generated takes of the run's other videos**, under headings of their own (the other videos' other files come with the tick, their originals never); **Other generations from another run…** picks a run and one of its videos and adds that video's generated takes to both pickers; either side can take an upload or a typed path; Video A still starts on this video's first generated take, never on the original. The original comes only from the run's own records (`runs.video_originals`): the source clip the video's requests or `state.json` name (`ref`, `reference`, the meaning of the first video input, `source_prepared`, the Recreate pick, the video's own entry: `V08`) as the run's inventory holds it (`00-inventory/inventory.json`, else `10-media/V08/video.json`), labelled `original · <file> (V08, the source clip as given)`, and the source as each generation sent it to the model (`source_prepared`, the first video input, `source`, the Video 1 entries of `50-requests/video-NN-uploads.json`), labelled `original as sent with a1, a2 · <file>` by the request files that sent it (`video-03-c1-a2-request.json` is `c1-a2`; the uploads record names it only when no request does); a byte-for-byte copy is listed once. A Create video, or a run whose records name no source, has none: nothing is guessed from a name or from the order of a list. `GET /api/runs` serves them with the role `original`. Each frame's tag says what its video is (`misses.side_desc`, from `runs.origin`: one of the report video's originals, else where the file sits in a run): `as generated` / `correct version` for the video's own files, uploads and typed paths, `original video` for an original, and `· video 04` or `· run R video 03` added for a take of another video or run; the words are kept with each frame of the moment (`frames[].desc`), so the QC library, the keeper's digest and the brief never read the run again, and each frame's own source picker on the page says the same (`Video A · original video`). `GET /api/request-prompt` and the product sample read a take of another video or run in its own run (that video's request); the original fills no prompt and says it was not generated.

**The product sample of every pipeline's runs (since 2026-10-27).** The finder reads what a request says of each input in either form: a `medias_meaning` table, or the meaning written on each input of `params.medias` (as `/analyze-with-loupe` writes it: "Image 2: the sealed … can (A14 front, …)"), and either registry form: `assets.products` (with `canonical` and `files`, or a `label`, `views` and `geometry`; a variant entry such as P01-WM is named with its product P01), or `assets.product` with each product's variants (P1 → P1-CC with its flavour, its label image as the canonical one and its views; an unused variant left out); one picture per product; and when a request describes none of its inputs, the video's uploads record (`50-requests/video-NN-uploads.json`: a key such as `a1 Image 2: P01-CC front padded` and its file) names each image input. The sample is named in the request's own words, and a new report's product field takes it until you type your own. The run's product names come from that registry before its `registry.md`.

**One call at a time, never stuck.** The keeper runs in its own process group and is ended whole when its call times out (on Windows `claude.cmd` starts node; killing only the shell left node holding the pipes and the lock, and one real consult waited 63 minutes). The lock around every keeper call is acquired with a timeout (`LOUPE_KEEPER_LOCK_WAIT`, default 120 s; a sync waits `LOUPE_KEEPER_SYNC_LOCK_WAIT`, 20 s): a consult that cannot get it answers from the brief with `mode: busy`, a sync reports `state: busy` and the watcher tries again. `GET /api/qc/keeper/state` (qc:read) returns in_sync, turns, the library version, the model and its effort, and the sync times, without the consult log or the session id.

**One Loupe per port (since 2026-10-28).** On Windows `SO_REUSEADDR` let a second server bind a port the first one already listened on, and the two split the requests: a test's server for its temp data (started by `loupe_client.ensure()` when a health check of the test's own server timed out) sat on 8787 beside the real one for 35 minutes, and the runs' keys were refused on about every other call. The server now binds without it on Windows (`OnePortServer`), so a second one stops with a message and leaves its `server.json` alone; and `ensure()` starts a server for a data folder other than Loupe's own (a test) only on that folder's own port (its `server.json`, else `LOUPE_PORT`), never on 8787, and otherwise reports Loupe unavailable.

**Always current.** The keeper is not left to wake at the first question: `POST /api/qc/keeper/sync` (what `atp.py|atg.py loupe ensure` calls when a run is created, resumed or about to spawn its agents) boots it when there is no session or the session is worn out, sends it only the changed lines when the library changed since its last digest (a fresh session when much changed), and does nothing (no model call) when it is current. The server also boots it at start-up and a watcher re-reads the library about 25 seconds after the admin's last edit (a run of edits costs one call), retrying a failure after five minutes. `GET /api/qc/keeper` and the Keeper page show `in_sync`. Each sync is in the audit log (`keeper.sync`, with its cost). A consult still checks the version itself, so a question never meets an outdated keeper.

**What the collector saw in the pictures (since 2026-10-22; a collector with no verdict since 2026-10-28).** The keeper used to be told each sample in words, with the path of its picture to Read, and in practice it almost never opened one. Now a separate, fresh, read-only session, the data collector (`keeper_extract_model`, default `claude-haiku-5-5`, changeable on the Keeper page), opens each sample ONCE: the composite with its WRONG / RIGHT / EXAMPLE frames and the admin's marks, the single frames when a detail is small, and the admin's words. It collects what they show and gives no verdict, and it is never given the prompts of the takes (the user, 2026-10-28: a reader that drew its own conclusions from the prompts had put a verdict where the judge decides). It must open the picture: a reply given without a tool call is asked once more, and a collection that still opened nothing is kept marked as such. Its collection (what each frame shows, what the marks point at, the right frame, the product and scene, the admin's words, what was unclear in the picture, and whether prompts are saved in the record) is kept as one JSON file per sample in `data/keeper/understood/S<id>.json`, with the signature of what it rests on (the admin's words, the frames, the picture's exact modification time and size, and the collector's version; the prompts are no part of it). Only a sample that is new, or that changed since its reading, is read again; when the readings match the library one for one nothing runs; a sample that leaves the library has its reading moved to `understood/retired/` and gets it back when it returns unchanged (no new reading); a reading that fails twice for the same content is given up (`understood/failed/`; the digest and the Keeper page say COULD NOT BE READ) until the sample changes or the admin asks again, so a failing sample never costs a call per tick. The judge's digest carries a short form of every collection on its sample's line (READ (collected from its picture, no verdict): what the marks point at, the right frame, the product and scene, each cut at a sentence, and the path of the full JSON; or NOT READ YET); its role tells it that this is data, not a judgement (what went wrong, why, when it recurs, what a check compares and which take or prompt did better are its own to judge from the picture, the admin's words and the record), to let the admin's words win where they differ, to Read the full collection when a judgement rests on what a frame shows or what was unclear, and to Read the picture itself when it rests on a detail the collection does not state. The server's watcher reads the new or changed samples before it tells the keeper (a failure is tried again after fifteen minutes, and the keeper is told meanwhile); `python loupe.py keeper understand [--ids S3]` and `POST /api/qc/keeper/understand` (admin) do it on demand, `python loupe.py keeper status` and `GET /api/qc/keeper/understanding` (admin) list the readings, `GET /api/qc/keeper/state` (qc:read) carries only the counts, and the Keeper page shows every reading with its picture and a button to read a sample again. Every keeper prompt now goes to the CLI on stdin: a digest with the readings is longer than a Windows command line may be.

**Memory rot (since 2026-10-22).** A keeper session is its whole history, so its context is kept small and current: the library goes in whole only when a session starts; after that a change sends only the lines that changed (a sample added, edited or whose image was rebuilt) and the ids that left, never the whole list again; a change to more than a third of the library (and more than three lines) starts a fresh session instead; and a session is replaced by a fresh one after 30 turns or once 150 000 characters of prompts and answers have gone through it (`ROT_CHARS`), whichever comes first. The keeper state keeps a mark per sample line (`lines`, never shown) and the characters carried (`chars`, in `GET /api/qc/keeper/state`). Each collection is made by its own fresh session that sees one sample only, and the pipeline's agents get from the keeper only what their question needs (a prompt template, or at most three short hints for a plan; the matching samples for QC), never the library or the collections. Every Read the keeper makes counts toward `ROT_CHARS` too (6 000 characters each, counted from the call's turns), because an opened picture or file fills the session as much as text does. Its thinking counts as well (4 characters per thinking token, from the call's usage): a resumed session keeps its earlier thinking, and a higher effort thinks longer.

**Coupled with the pipelines (since 2026-10-22).** The keeper asks instead of guessing, and what it asks reaches someone. A QC answer's `relevant[]` carries each sample's `reading` (what the collector saw: what the marks point at and the right frame, cut, and whether it opened the picture) and `reading_path` (the whole collection, read on demand); a planning hint and a template's basis carry `reading_path`; every answer carries `library_read` (how many samples the keeper holds from their pictures, and which it judged from the admin's words alone). A question the keeper asks that Loupe cannot fetch comes back as `needs_unmet` to the agent who asked (the QC agent answers from its video, the prompt writer from its plan) and is answered once with a note; the reason says which of the two it is. Each consult's record keeps, whole and apart from the cut answer, what the keeper asked for, could not get, held back, judged from words alone and found the library does not cover (`consults.asks`); `GET /api/qc/keeper` returns it per consult and the Keeper page shows it in the consult table and in a card, What the keeper is missing, so the admin can add a sample where the library has a gap. `POST /api/qc/keeper/sync` (what a run start calls) also starts reading the new or changed samples in the background and answers `understanding` and `reading_started`.

**Asking instead of guessing.** The digest line of a sample says where it came from (run, video, time, mode) and leaves empty fields out. The keeper may put a `needs` list in its answer: `video_plan`, `request_prompt`, `product_registry`, `video_frame` (QC only, with a time), `sample_origin` (a sample id: its plan, request prompt and product image) or `ask_agent` (a question only the QC agent can answer). Loupe fetches what it can from the run named in the question, read-only and capped, copies it into `data/keeper/ctx/<consult>/` (the last 20 are kept), sends it to the same session and the keeper answers again; at most two rounds of three requests, each round one keeper turn. What cannot be fetched comes back as `needs_unmet`; the toolkits print an `unmet_rule` and the QC agent answers once with `qc-consult --note`. Fetched data is evidence for that one answer: it is never added to the library, the keeper is told so, and the library stays its only memory.

## Planning hints (the keeper's second job)

The first request of each video asks the keeper once, in text only, which past misses could matter for its plan (`plan-hints --beats …`).
The keeper is told to be sparse: the default answer is no hints, a hint needs a beat of that plan that matches the sample's trigger and a
compatible model, at most three, each a risk plus a positive lever (what the right version shows and which of the writer's own tools to use at
that beat), never a wording to paste. Loupe enforces the same in code (real ids, high or medium confidence, model family must fit unless the
miss is model-independent, three at most, fields cut at a sentence end (600 characters for the trigger and the outcome, 700 for the lever, 500 for the caveat: the condition that decides whether a hint applies stays whole), no images, nothing at all when the keeper is unavailable or busy). `held_back_why` says why each offered hint was set aside. The writer's rules are in the
`atp-write-request` and `atg-write-request` skills: apply only to the matching beat, smallest change, positive wording, at most two per video, record the decisions.
/analyze-with-loupe asks for the judge's prompt template instead (below).

## The prompt template (the judge's third job, since 2026-10-28)

The user, 2026-10-28: the judge and the agents write an accurate prompt from what already worked and what failed in earlier generations, rather than
only inspecting one. `POST /api/qc/consult` with `purpose: template` (`awl.py prompt-template`, X4b: after the pipeline planned the video, wrote its own request and checked
it) takes the whole checked brief (the agent's words: what it is working on, what the video is about, what it wants to achieve; the checked draft
request's prompt, parameters and inputs; the lines the prompt must carry word for word; 20000 characters at most), the locked model's request
structure (the pattern card, `structure`) and the generation video model's entry of the pipeline's model index (`video_model`). Step 1: the judge names the
samples whose saved prompts it needs (at most six, `read`); step 2: Loupe sends both prompts of each whole and the judge answers `template` (the FINAL
prompt, which the pipeline puts in the request word for word: built on the checked draft, every must-carry line word for word, within the service's
limit, in that model's structure, for this video, carrying the wording and the mechanism that made a corrected take work where the situation is the same,
each failure designed out in positive words, `[SLOT: …]` only for a value the brief does not give), `basis` (per sample: what worked, what failed, which
part of the template carries it; only samples that exist), `notes`, `changes` (parts of the brief earlier generations say should change) and
`not_covered`; it may ask with `needs` as in any consult. The pipeline never rewrites it (`awl.py template-apply` puts it in the
request and reports the must-carry lines missing, the slots left and the length; a missing line or a length over the limit is asked of the judge once
more, and only then is the line added word for word or the checked draft kept); the judge is not asked again before the generation. After the generation, a QC consult carries what the judge needs to judge an error accurately (`request_prompt`, the
prompt the video was generated from; `request_params`, that request's model and parameters; `video_model`; `template`; `brief`), and the QC answer adds
`cause` (which part of the prompt or of the template let the error happen) and `template_fix` (that part rewritten). The judge weighs every sample by
the video model that generated it. The consult log shows the purpose as "prompt template".

The keeper matches by mechanism: both consult kinds carry the product's details (shape, size, opening, natural posture, from its registry row,
`--product-details`), a sample is relevant when it and the video share a product detail and a scene mechanism, and its answers are conditions
for this video, never "never/always" commands (Loupe also holds back a hint worded that way). Fill the report form's product field with the
product's details, not only its name, so samples can be matched this way.

# Loupe

The web app of the video pipelines (`/analyze-then-pick`, `/analyze-then-generate` and `/analyze-with-loupe`; the runs of all three can be browsed here). It does three things:

1. **Flag record.** Every prompt and video Higgsfield flagged, refused, withheld or rerouted, with the evidence the pipeline could
   gather (the request, each input with its file, thumbnail and file facts, the same file in earlier runs, the raw answer the
   hook saved). It replaces the old `caught_flags.html` page.
2. **Generation misses.** One page, no second step: you pick a run and a video, the frames play side by side (two to start with: the miss from Video A and the right version from Video B, on the same time; either can also be the original video the generation was made from, or a take of another video or run; choosing the right version later, even after marks were drawn on the first frame, adds its frame once at the same time, and the marks stay on the frame they were drawn on), and you
   draw an arrow, circle, box or note straight on any of them (red on a frame you call Wrong, green on a frame you call Right). You choose what each
   frame is: Wrong or Right, which video it comes from (so an early frame of one video can be the right one), and its own time (slider, 1-frame buttons or a time box); **+ Add a frame** adds as many as you need.
   Nothing is marked or copied for you. Undo / Redo / Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y work. Everything is optional: a right version, a wrong frame, even a video or any frame (a report can be in words only: the idea, what could have been better, what the AI agents missed or could not understand).
   Type what the miss was or what the scenario is about,
   press **Mark this moment** (it saves at once), and Loupe saves one image: each frame
   tagged WRONG, RIGHT or EXAMPLE with its time and where it comes from, a zoom of each frame you marked with a circle, arrow or box (cut around that frame's own marks; a frame with no such mark has none), and your explanation burned in at the bottom. Your marks are drawn on the zoom only: a frame that has a zoom stays clean above it, and a frame with no zoom (a text label only, marks over most of it) keeps its marks on the frame, so no mark is lost; the full-size `frame-N-marked.png` files keep every frame's marks in place. The bottom band is split in half: the explanation on
   the left and, on the right, the **images sent with the video's generation**, as the run recorded them (the request the ledger submitted,
   unchanged since, and the file uploaded under each media id), every one by default: the report page lists them ticked, and you leave any out or switch the band off. A miss does not need a right version:
   the page never guesses the right version: you pick it, or pick "No right version exists", before a moment can be saved, and then the miss stays as a miss example (the right panel says so, only the miss is marked). Attach a right version
   later and the saved moments are rebuilt with it (open Edit on each to line the right video up).
3. **QC samples.** The QC phase of a run (and only that phase) reads those images. A separate read-only Claude session, the *keeper*,
   holds the whole library and answers QC agents' questions about it; it never edits anything.

Standard library Python + SQLite + Pillow + ffmpeg. The UI is plain JavaScript (no innerHTML). It listens on `127.0.0.1:8787` only.

## Who may do what

| Who | How | Scopes |
|---|---|---|
| You | passphrase login (12 h session cookie, CSRF header, Host check, 5 wrong tries lock the login for a minute) | everything |
| The pipeline (`atp.py`, `atg.py`, `awl.py`, hooks) | `Authorization: Bearer lk_…` from `data/keys/pipeline.key` | `flags:write`, `flags:read`, `runs:read` |
| The QC phase and the judge (`qc-consult`, `qc-brief` and `plan-hints` of each script; `awl.py prompt-template`, `sample-prompts`, the keeper's sync and state; MCP) | `data/keys/qc.key` | `qc:read`, `flags:read` |

Keys are shown once, stored hashed, revocable, with a last-used time and a use counter; logins, key changes, flag and miss edits, settings
and uploads go to the audit log (Settings page); consults are logged on the Keeper page. Make more with `python loupe.py key-create --name N --scopes a,b [--file NAME]`.

## How agents reach it

- **CLI (primary):** `caught-flag add|set|list|reconcile|open`, `qc-consult`, `qc-brief`, `plan-hints` and `loupe ensure|status|open`, with the same arguments in `atp.py`, `atg.py` and `awl.py` (each writes the runs of its own library). `awl.py` also reads the keeper's state (`GET /api/qc/keeper/state`, qc key) in its `status` and `doctor`, and uploads from a capture (`hf-put --capture`).
- **MCP (optional):** `mcp_server.py` offers `qc_search`, `qc_get_sample` (text + the image), `qc_brief`, `qc_consult`, `flags_search`.
  Register once with `claude mcp add --scope user loupe -- python C:/Users/Glaresquare1/.claude/loupe/mcp_server.py`
  (it reads `data/keys/qc.key`, or `LOUPE_API_KEY` if you pass `-e LOUPE_API_KEY=<key>`).
- **HTTP:** the same JSON API the web page uses (`/api/...`, listed in `loupe_app/server.py`).

**Kinds suggested, confirmed by the admin.** `GET /api/misses/suggest-kinds?text=…` returns the kinds of the vocabulary whose own words occur in a text (a prefix match on the vocabulary's words, nothing weighed); the report form's button "Suggest kinds from the words" ticks them and the admin unticks what does not fit; nothing is saved without the admin.

**The composite says what its tags mean.** When the frames come from two sources, or from another video or run, the bottom band opens with a legend, one clause per tag (frames tagged "as generated" are from video A, "correct version" from video B, a different video, "original video" from the original the generation was made from, not generated; "… · video 04" or "… · run R video 03" says where a take of another video or run was taken from); it reads exactly as before for the two usual tags. A frame gets a zoom only when you marked it with a circle, an arrow or a box, cut around its own marks: a frame with no mark, or with a text label only, has none (its place under the row stays empty, and a row with no zoom has no zoom row), and neither has a frame whose own marks cover more than 60 % of it (it would repeat the panel; the band says so). A source too long for its panel shrinks the tag's letters, then is shortened, so the time always shows. Existing samples keep their images until they are regenerated.

**Edited where it stands; the product sample ticked by the admin (since 2026-10-28).** A report and its samples are changed in place, never started again: a sample opened from the reports list, or with **Edit frames and marks** on the QC samples page (`&moment=` in the workspace's address), comes back with its frames, their times and roles, its marks and its words, ready for **Update moment**; a report with one sample opens it that way; **Save report changes** saves the report's own fields (what the miss or scenario is about, the kinds, the videos, the product sample, the prompts, model, mode, product) and rebuilds every image of the report without marking a new moment; and the QC samples page edits the report's title beside the sample's words. The band's images are ticked by the admin from the images the generation of Video A's take was sent (`GET /api/generation-assets`, `runs.generation_assets`, from `runs.sent_images`: see Only the images sent with the generation, below), each with a thumbnail, every one ticked by default. The ticked files, up to nine, are kept with the report (`misses.sample_files`, added by a migration; a file that is not one of the images that generation was sent is refused, and a tick on a client's original stands for the file sent from it) and go into its images; untouched, every image sent is used. A frame whose video the browser cannot play is shown as stills that Loupe cuts (`/api/frame`), so it can still be moved, marked and saved; a time past the end of a shorter take is brought back to its last frame; a frame asked for near the end of a file whose sound runs longer than its picture steps back to the nearest picture; and a take whose run cannot be read keeps its side's tag instead of stopping the save.

## The keeper

The keeper is the judge (the user, 2026-10-28). `qc.py` starts `claude -p … --model claude-opus-5-5 --effort high --tools Read --strict-mcp-config`
(`keeper_model` on the Keeper page, Opus 5.5 unless the admin sets another) as a session of its own (`--session-id`, then `--resume`), working in
`data/keeper`, told the whole library digest. It can read the sample images and write nothing. It alone judges: what went wrong, why, what a check
compares, which prompt did better, what a template should say; the data collector (below) only describes the pictures. After 30 turns it is replaced by a
fresh session rebuilt from the library (the library is the memory, so nothing is lost). If it cannot run, `qc-consult` falls back to the deterministic
brief (`mode: fallback`). A consult on Opus costs more than it did on Haiku and grows with the library and the session's length; each consult's cost is
kept in the `consults` table of the database. Every judge and collector call is started with `--effort` at high or above (`keeper_effort` on the Keeper
page: high, xhigh or max; a lower value is refused, and one stored around the API runs at high), because the Haiku collector's own default is medium;
the CLI still runs a model that has no effort levels.

**What an answer carries (since 2026-10-20).** Every `relevant[]` item names the sample's `title` (the admin's words), `details`, `correct_note`, `idea`, `better`, `missed`, where it came from (`model`, `run`, `video`, `t_wrong`, `kinds`), its prompt boxes (`prompts`, `prompt_roles`, `prompt_sides`), the composite's path and the keeper's `why`, `check` and `wrong_pattern`; the brief's `samples` (`qc-brief`, and a consult answered `busy` or `fallback`) carry the same fields but the keeper's, with what the collector saw (`reading`, `reading_path`). Every field is whole or cut at a sentence end with an ellipsis (`_clip`), never mid-word; `needs_unmet` lists only what Loupe could not fetch, and its `more` item names the kinds and times the keeper still wanted. A run that is not under Loupe's run folders is named as such in the fetch reason.

**The prompts of the two boxes (since 2026-10-20).** Beside Video A and Video B the report form keeps the flawed version's prompt and the correct version's prompt (each filled from the run's request files of the generations the frames come from, or pasted; see the box rule below) and one line of the admin's own words on what in the prompt made the difference. They live in the `misses` table only (`wrong_prompt`, `fixed_prompt`, `prompt_note`; the seed carries them): never in the composite, its meta or the keeper's digest, and nothing compares or diffs them in code. The judge reads them whole, never cut (the user, 2026-10-28): when it asks for `sample_origin`, in a template request for the samples it chooses, and, before any QC or planning answer is final, for the samples the answer cites that it has not read in that consult, up to ten in the order cited (`PROMPTS_READ`; `prompts_judged` names every cited sample whose prompts it read whole in that consult, `prompts_unread` any it did not); it judges itself what the correct version's prompt did differently. The data collector never sees them. A pipeline agent reads them through `GET /api/qc/samples/S<id>?prompts=1`: `awl.py qc-consult`, `plan-hints` and `prompt-template` write them into the run for every sample the answer rests on (`prompts_files`), and `awl.py sample-prompts` fetches any other one. Every list, brief, consult answer and hint carries only `prompts` (`pair`, `wrong`, `fixed` or empty) and `prompt_note`, so the keeper's memory and the agents' context stay small; a change to the prompts changes the library version, so the keeper is re-synced and re-reads them on request.

**A take made of several generations (since 2026-10-28; read from the real join since 2026-10-29).** A video joined from clips (`60-outputs/video-NN/c1/`, `c2/` …) was made by one generation per clip, each with its own request. `runs.prompt_segments` gives one segment per generation, numbered in clip order (`gen` of `of`), with its clip, the attempt that went in, its request (`video-NN-cK[-aJ]-request.json`, else the request whose `clip` key names it), its prompt, whole, and every span of seconds of that version it made (`spans`; `t_from`/`t_to` are the first one): a clip that comes back after another one (c1, c2, then c1 again) is one generation with two spans, and seconds no generation made (an insert) are listed apart (`inserts`). Which seconds each generation made is read, never assumed (`runs._take_spans`), in this order, each used only when its pieces add up to the file's length within 0.1 s: the join record `awl.py concat` writes beside its output (`<file>.join.json`, matched on the file's own sha1, so a copy finds it; restore-audio, conform, clean, captions and trim carry it to their output); for a render, the video's CapCut project (its main track, when the project is unchanged since the file was written and as long as it); a concat list (ffmpeg's `file '...'` lines) beside a byte-for-byte copy of the file in an attempt folder (a clean video copied up from `a2/` keeps `a2/concat-list.txt`, never an older list beside the copy); a render of the whole clean video (as long as it, made after it); a concat list beside the file itself; the clips' own pieces (`c1/c1.mp4` …) in clip order; the clips' trimmed files (`trimmed.mp4`, else `conformed.mp4`, else `raw.mp4`). The attempt of a piece is the folder it sits in, else the attempt folder holding a byte-for-byte copy of it, else what the run's result says it shipped (`jobs[].shipped`), else the number of attempts the run made for it, else the highest attempt folder with a file. When nothing adds up, `spans_known` is false and no segment is given: which generation made a frame is then not known, and nothing is tied to it. A take of one clip gives that clip's own request, so a take of `c2` is never given `c1`'s prompt. `GET /api/request-prompt` answers a joined take with `segments` and `inserts`, and the product sample offers the images of every clip's request.

Each consult's record keeps, whole and apart from the cut answer, what the keeper asked for, could not get, held back, judged from words alone and found the library does not cover (`consults.asks`); `GET /api/qc/keeper` returns it per consult and the Keeper page shows it in the consult table and in a card, What the keeper is missing, so the admin can add a sample where the library has a gap. `POST /api/qc/keeper/sync` (what a run start calls) also starts reading the new or changed samples in the background and answers `understanding` and `reading_started`.

**Asking instead of guessing.** The digest line of a sample says where it came from (run, video, time, mode) and leaves empty fields out. The keeper may put a `needs` list in its answer: `video_plan`, `request_prompt`, `product_registry`, `video_frame` (QC only, with a time), `sample_origin` (a sample id: its plan, request prompt and product image) or `ask_agent` (a question only the QC agent can answer). Loupe fetches what it can from the run named in the question, read-only and capped, copies it into `data/keeper/ctx/<consult>/` (the last 20 are kept), sends it to the same session and the keeper answers again; at most two rounds of three requests, each round one keeper turn. What cannot be fetched comes back as `needs_unmet`; the toolkits print an `unmet_rule` and the QC agent answers once with `qc-consult --note`. Fetched data is evidence for that one answer: it is never added to the library, the keeper is told so, and the library stays its only memory.

## Planning hints (the keeper's second job)

The first request of each video asks the keeper once, in text only, which past misses could matter for its plan (`plan-hints --beats …`).
The keeper is told to be sparse: the default answer is no hints, a hint needs a beat of that plan that matches the sample's trigger and a
compatible model, at most three, each a risk plus a positive lever (what the right version shows and which of the writer's own tools to use at
that beat), never a wording to paste. Loupe enforces the same in code (real ids, high or medium confidence, model family must fit unless the
miss is model-independent, three at most, fields cut at a sentence end (600 characters for the trigger and the outcome, 700 for the lever, 500 for the caveat: the condition that decides whether a hint applies stays whole), no images, nothing at all when the keeper is unavailable or busy). `held_back_why` says why each offered hint was set aside. The writer's rules are in the
`atp-write-request` and `atg-write-request` skills: apply only to the matching beat, smallest change, positive wording, at most two per video, record the decisions.
/analyze-with-loupe asks for the judge's prompt template instead (below).

## The prompt template (the judge's third job, since 2026-10-28)

The user, 2026-10-28: the judge and the agents write an accurate prompt from what already worked and what failed in earlier generations, rather than
only inspecting one. `POST /api/qc/consult` with `purpose: template` (`awl.py prompt-template`, X4b: after the pipeline planned the video, wrote its own request and checked
it) takes the whole checked brief (the agent's words: what it is working on, what the video is about, what it wants to achieve; the checked draft
request's prompt, parameters and inputs; the lines the prompt must carry word for word; whole, never cut), the locked model's request
structure (the pattern card, `structure`) and the generation video model's entry of the pipeline's model index (`video_model`). Step 1: the judge names the
samples whose saved prompts it needs (at most ten, `read`, `TEMPLATE_READ`); step 2: Loupe sends the saved prompts of each whole (every box its frames speak through, named flawed, correct or example) and the judge answers `template` (the FINAL
prompt, which the pipeline puts in the request word for word: built on the checked draft, every must-carry line word for word, within the service's
limit, in that model's structure, for this video, carrying the wording and the mechanism that made a correct version work where the situation is the same,
each failure designed out in positive words, `[SLOT: …]` only for a value the brief does not give), `basis` (per sample: what worked, what failed, which
part of the template carries it; only samples that exist), `notes`, `changes` (parts of the brief earlier generations say should change) and
`not_covered`; it may ask with `needs` as in any consult. It also answers `product_images`: which of the client's images of the product the
request carries and how many (the brief lists each input with the client's original it was made from and the client's images of that product;
see the product images below). The pipeline never rewrites it (`awl.py template-apply` puts it in the
request and reports the must-carry lines missing, the slots left and the length; a missing line or a length over the limit is asked of the judge once
more, and only then is the line added word for word or the checked draft kept); the judge is not asked again before the generation. After the generation, a QC consult carries what the judge needs to judge an error accurately (`request_prompt`, the
prompt the video was generated from; `request_params`, that request's model and parameters; `request_inputs`, its inputs with the client's original
each was made from and its product images; `video_model`; `template`; `brief`), and the QC answer adds `cause` (which part of the prompt or of the
template let the error happen) and `template_fix` (that part rewritten), and, when the error comes from the product images, `product_images` for
the regeneration. The judge weighs every sample by
the video model that generated it. The consult log shows the purpose as "prompt template".

The keeper matches by mechanism: both consult kinds carry the product's details (shape, size, opening, natural posture, from its registry row,
`--product-details`), a sample is relevant when it and the video share a product detail and a scene mechanism, and its answers are conditions
for this video, never "never/always" commands (Loupe also holds back a hint worded that way). Fill the report form's product field with the
product's details, not only its name, so samples can be matched this way.
